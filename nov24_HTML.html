Trademarks of the '20s-'40s

It seems like this is a multi-volume book series about graphic design. What I like is that it serves as an archive for an older sense of aesthetic.

Whenever I see old local businesses, I always feel like I prefer that aesthetic over the hyper-simplistic look of the 2020s.

I think a notable example of the design shift in local businesses is that nowadays, you rarely see any with a hanging sign on the outside.

I think that's a shame. I think hanging signs add a lot of character to the exterior.

Graphic Design: Visual Comparisons

I like the variety of graphic design this book goes over.

It shows that it's more than just drawing a picture.

But despite that, the book does also go over depictions.

And I like that you can learn how to design things in a way that's both market-practical, and unique to yourself!

Overall, I wonder. If you were to end up in the Library of Babel, how long would it take to find a book on graphic design?

Reading that also reminded me of recurring dream I had when I was 8 years old.

I'd walk into the local CVS, but just to then be met with an endlessly-expanding ornate library. There was also a regular old cash register.
